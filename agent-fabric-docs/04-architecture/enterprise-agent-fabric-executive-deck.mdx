# Enterprise Agent Fabric

## Executive quick deck

**One governed front door for enterprise AI**

**Status:** Draft · **Date:** 2 September 2026

Full decision paper: [Enterprise Agent Fabric V1](./enterprise-agent-fabric-pitch.mdx) · Technical design: [Enterprise Agent Fabric](./enterprise-agent-fabric-architecture.mdx)

---

## Slide 1 — What it is

The **Enterprise Agent Fabric** is a shared operating layer for enterprise AI.

It is **not** one giant agent. It is the infrastructure that sits between users and domain-owned AI capabilities.

- **One front door** — one entry point for employees, customers, applications, and jobs
- **Many specialised capabilities** — Finance, HR, Legal, Claims, Payments, and others stay domain-owned
- **One control model** — identity, entitlement, policy, audit, monitoring, and evaluation applied consistently

**In one line:** scale AI as a governed portfolio, not as a collection of disconnected applications.

---

## Slide 2 — The problem we are solving

AI capabilities are emerging independently in every domain.

- **Employee** — Finance Assistant, HR Assistant, Legal Assistant, Technology Assistant
- **Customer** — Customer Service AI, Claims AI
- **Partner** — Payments AI, KYC AI

Each team rebuilds its own front door, auth, routing, audit, and ops.

The organisation loses one answer to:

- **Who** may use this capability?
- **What** is it allowed to do?
- **Why** was it selected, and what did it do?

## Slide 3 — Why now

AI is moving from answering questions to **performing work and taking actions**.

That raises the stakes:

- More integration with enterprise data, customers, money, and operations
- More need for approval, audit, and accountability
- More risk if every team invents its own controls

Without a common operating model, each new capability adds duplication, inconsistency, and governance overhead.

**The shift:** from “build another AI app” to “onboard another governed capability.”

---

## Slide 4 — What we propose

A **shared operating layer** between users and specialised AI.

The Fabric handles:

- **Who** may act — identity and entitlement
- **Which** capability should respond — routing and policy
- **How** work runs safely — audit, approval, monitoring, and ops

Domains keep ownership of workflows, knowledge, models, and business outcomes.

## Slide 5 — What you get

- **One AI experience** — users do not need to know which assistant or endpoint to use
- **Faster onboarding** — register a governed capability instead of rebuilding platform plumbing
- **Consistent controls** — entitlement, policy, kill switch, and least-privilege once for all capabilities
- **Clear accountability** — one evidence model across the portfolio
- **Operational visibility** — shared health and failure view across the platform
- **Safer change** — routing and behaviour checked before production, not only after incidents
- **Failure isolation** — one capability failing does not take down enterprise AI
- **Domain autonomy** — business teams keep speed and specialisation; the platform owns the common path

**Outcome:** scale AI adoption without scaling fragmentation and risk at the same rate.

---

## Slide 6 — How it works

Every request follows the same governed path:

1. **One experience** — enter through the enterprise front door
2. **Governed decision** — understand intent, select capability, verify entitlement and policy
3. **Specialised capability** — domain AI runs with approved data and tools
4. **Controlled action** — high-risk steps gated by policy, approval, or human handoff
5. **Auditable outcome** — record what was decided, executed, and approved

Chat and automated jobs use the same contract. Different ingress paths can scale independently.

## Slide 7 — Benefits and how

| Benefit | How the Fabric delivers it |
| --- | --- |
| One AI experience | Common front door and capability catalogue — users ask the enterprise, not a specific agent |
| Faster onboarding | Register capabilities to a shared contract instead of rebuilding auth, routing, audit, and ops |
| Consistent controls | One entitlement, policy, and least-privilege model — separate user, capability, and runtime permissions |
| Clear accountability | Append-only audit evidence — who asked, what was selected, what ran, what was approved |
| Operational visibility | Shared observability across Front Door, Plane, and Runtime |
| Safer change | Evaluation gates on routing and capability behaviour before release |
| Failure isolation | Independent scaling for chat, API, and capabilities — one outage stays local |
| Domain autonomy | Platform owns the common path; domains own specialised workflows and outcomes |

---

## Slide 9 — What it is not

- Not one giant enterprise agent
- Not a replacement for domain AI teams or platforms
- Not one model or one runtime for everything
- Not central ownership of every use case

**Centralise** what must be consistent: identity, routing, policy, audit, observability, evaluation, reliability.

**Decentralise** what domains do best: business logic, workflows, knowledge, and specialised quality.

---

## Slide 10 — The ask

Endorse the **Enterprise Agent Fabric** as the enterprise pattern for governed AI.

Support **V1** as the foundation to:

- Harden the platform for production
- Onboard priority capabilities across domains
- Establish enterprise standards for controls, audit, and evaluation

**Decision:** scale AI as a governed capability portfolio — or keep scaling disconnected AI applications independently.
