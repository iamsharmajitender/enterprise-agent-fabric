# Enterprise Agent Fabric

## Executive Brief

### One governed way to run enterprise AI

**2 September 2026**

[Full decision paper](./enterprise-agent-fabric-pitch.mdx) · [Technical design](./enterprise-agent-fabric-architecture.mdx)

---

## Why leadership should care

AI is no longer a set of pilots in individual teams. It is becoming **production capability** across the organisation — for employees, customers, partners, and operations.

That is the right direction. The risk is **how** we scale it.

If every division builds its own AI entry point, security model, approval path, and audit trail, we do not get more innovation. We get **the same enterprise plumbing rebuilt many times** — with different standards, different gaps, and no single place to answer hard questions when something goes wrong.

We need to move from *“another AI application”* to *“another governed capability in the enterprise portfolio.”*

---

## What is happening today

Capabilities are emerging independently:

- **Employees** — finance, HR, legal, technology assistants
- **Customers** — service and claims AI
- **Partners** — payments and KYC AI

Each team solves the visible problem — the assistant or automation — and often rebuilds the invisible work underneath: who can access it, how requests are directed, what the AI is allowed to do, how actions are approved, and how we prove what happened afterwards.

The organisation cannot consistently answer:

- **Who** was allowed to use this AI?
- **What** was it permitted to do?
- **Why** was this capability chosen, and what did it actually do?

## What we are proposing

**Enterprise Agent Fabric** is the common operating layer for AI across the enterprise.

It is **not** one giant AI that does everything. It is the **enterprise infrastructure** that sits between users and the specialised AI each domain builds.

**For users and applications:** one place to ask for help.

**For the enterprise:** one consistent way to control access, direct work, govern actions, monitor health, and retain evidence.

**For domain teams:** freedom to build specialised AI for their business — without rebuilding the enterprise control stack every time.

---

## What the enterprise standardises once

These are not technical details for their own sake. They are the controls every production AI capability will need anyway.

- **Access** — who may use which AI, under which role and entitlement
- **Direction** — which specialist capability handles each request, under defined rules
- **Action** — what the AI may do, including approvals for higher-risk steps
- **Evidence** — a consistent record of what was requested, decided, executed, and approved
- **Oversight** — operational visibility and pre-production checks before change reaches users

We build this once at enterprise level. Domains plug into it instead of reinventing it.

---

## What domains keep

This proposal does **not** centralise business AI or slow domain teams down.

Domains continue to own:

- The business problem and workflow
- Domain knowledge and data
- Models, tools, and specialist logic
- Business outcomes and quality for their use cases

> **Centralise the controls. Keep the intelligence in the domains.**

That is the balance: **enterprise consistency without enterprise bottleneck.**

---

## What the organisation gains

- **Move faster over time** — new AI ships by onboarding to a shared model, not by standing up another full stack
- **Reduce risk** — the same security, approval, and policy standards apply everywhere AI acts on behalf of the business
- **Improve accountability** — leadership and regulators get a clear trail: who asked, what ran, what was approved, what happened
- **Scale without chaos** — the AI portfolio can grow without an equal growth in duplicated platform work, inconsistent controls, and operational blind spots

---

## The shift we are asking for

| Today | With Enterprise Agent Fabric |
| --- | --- |
| Each team builds, secures, and operates AI on its own | The enterprise provides one governed path; domains focus on specialist capability |
| Adding AI means adding another application and another control stack | Adding AI means onboarding another capability to a known operating model |
| Hard to explain the AI landscape or prove control after an incident | One catalogue, one evidence model, one operational view |

---

## What V1 delivers

V1 is deliberately bounded. It establishes the **pattern**, not a monolithic AI platform.

Leadership endorsement would allow us to:

1. **Harden for production** — security, resilience, and operational readiness
2. **Onboard priority capabilities** — prove the model on real business use cases
3. **Set enterprise standards** — access, audit, monitoring, and release discipline
4. **Create a repeatable path** — so the next AI capability is faster and safer than the last

V1 does **not** require one model, one runtime, or one team owning every use case.

---

## The decision

**Option A:** Continue letting AI scale as independent applications — accepting duplication, inconsistency, and growing governance exposure.

**Option B:** Endorse **Enterprise Agent Fabric** as the enterprise operating model for AI — one governed front door, many specialised capabilities, one consistent control model.

**Recommendation:** proceed with **Enterprise Agent Fabric V1**.

The goal is not to build one AI to rule them all. The goal is to give the organisation **one disciplined way to run many AIs** — with the speed domains need and the control the enterprise requires.

---
