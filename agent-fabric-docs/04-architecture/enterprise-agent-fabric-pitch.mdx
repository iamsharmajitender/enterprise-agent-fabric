# Enterprise Agent Fabric V1

## Executive Decision Paper

**One governed front door for enterprise AI**

**Status:** Draft · **Date:** 2 September 2026

Technical design: [Enterprise Agent Fabric](./enterprise-agent-fabric-architecture.mdx)

---

## 1. Executive decision requested

Endorse the **Enterprise Agent Fabric** as the strategic enterprise pattern for accessing, governing, and operating AI capabilities, and support V1 as the foundation for onboarding priority AI capabilities across business domains.

That decision means the Fabric becomes the common enterprise pattern for AI capability access and orchestration; business and domain teams retain ownership of their specialised capabilities while the Fabric supplies common enterprise controls; new enterprise AI capabilities should, where appropriate, be onboarded through the Fabric rather than as disconnected applications; V1 should proceed toward production hardening and onboarding of selected priority capabilities; and enterprise governance, security, risk, and technology teams establish the control model and standards.

The strategic choice is simple: do we scale AI as a portfolio of governed enterprise capabilities, or continue scaling individual AI applications independently? The Enterprise Agent Fabric provides the foundation for the former.

---

## 2. Executive summary

Enterprise AI is moving from a small number of assistants and experiments to a portfolio of specialised capabilities across Finance, HR, Legal, Customer Service, Payments, Claims, KYC, Operations, and Technology. The challenge is no longer simply building an agent. It is operating many AI capabilities as one enterprise capability while maintaining security, entitlement, governance, auditability, resilience, and accountability.

The Enterprise Agent Fabric addresses this with one front door, many specialised capabilities, and one control model. Users and applications do not need to understand the underlying AI landscape. They enter through a common interface; the Fabric determines the appropriate capability, verifies entitlement and policy, and connects the request to the relevant specialised capability. The capability remains domain-owned and independently evolvable. The enterprise controls remain consistent.

---

## 3. Why now

Enterprise AI is moving from answering questions toward performing work and taking actions. That changes the architectural and governance problem. A standalone chatbot can operate with relatively limited integration. A portfolio of AI capabilities may interact with enterprise data, customer information, financial systems, operational platforms, APIs, workflow systems, human approval processes, and external services.

Without a common operating model, each new capability can introduce another implementation of authentication, authorisation, routing, integration, monitoring, audit, and risk controls. The result is increasing technology duplication, operational complexity, inconsistent controls, security risk, governance overhead, user confusion, and difficulty demonstrating accountability.

The organisation therefore needs to move from “how do we build another AI application?” to “how do we add another governed AI capability to the enterprise?”

---

## 4. The problem

Today, AI capabilities naturally emerge independently across business domains. Employees encounter Finance, HR, Legal, and Technology assistants; customers and partners encounter Customer Service, Claims, Payments, and KYC endpoints. The landscape fragments: users need to know which capability to use, and applications need to know which endpoint to call. Teams independently implement authentication, entitlement, routing, tool access, data access, monitoring, audit, approval, governance, and resilience.

The organisation loses a single answer to three fundamental questions: who may use this capability; what is the capability allowed to do; and why was this capability selected and what did it do?

## 5. The proposition

The Enterprise Agent Fabric provides a common operating layer for enterprise AI. Users and applications interact with the enterprise AI capability rather than individual agents. Business domains retain specialised AI for their own processes, data, and expertise. Identity, entitlement, policy, monitoring, audit, and governance apply consistently across capabilities.

The operating model is one experience, a governed decision, a specialised capability, a controlled action, and an auditable outcome.

## 7. How the Fabric works

A request enters through the Front Door, where identity is established. The decision layer understands intent, selects the appropriate capability, and verifies entitlement. The specialised capability then executes its workflow using approved knowledge, data, and tools, while security, policy, monitoring, governance, and audit apply throughout. The outcome is either a result returned to the caller or an approved business action.

Chat interactions and automated jobs use the same underlying contract: entitle, decide, pin, start, execute, and audit. Different ingress channels can operate independently while maintaining a consistent enterprise control model. Production splits chat and API ingress into separate fleets; the contract stays the same.

---

## 8. What changes for the business

Today, users choose individual AI applications through multiple gateways, each with domain-specific authentication and controls. Agents are deployed independently, auditability is inconsistent, the AI landscape is hard to see as a whole, and adding AI means standing up another application — often on shared infrastructure that creates large failure domains.

With the Enterprise Agent Fabric, users access one enterprise AI experience through a common front door. Controls become reusable, capabilities are registered and governed, audit and evidence are consistent, and a central catalogue makes the landscape visible. Adding AI becomes onboarding another capability, while capabilities and ingress can be scaled and isolated independently.

The desired shift is from many AI applications to one enterprise AI capability ecosystem.

---

## 9. Least-privilege AI

A fundamental principle of the Fabric is that user access does not automatically translate into unlimited AI access. Three identities and permissions must be considered independently: the user who initiated the request, the capability and what it is authorised to do, and the executing platform and what infrastructure and services the runtime is allowed to access.

This separation enables least-privilege AI. A user may be entitled to a Payments capability without the executing agent being authorised to perform every payment operation directly. The Fabric provides a control point for user entitlement, capability permissions, tool permissions, data access, action policies, and approval requirements. Technical detail: [three principals](./enterprise-agent-fabric-architecture.mdx#three-principals).

---

## 10. Human control by design

Enterprise AI must maintain a clear distinction between answering and acting. Process gates pause a run before a risky action, such as a refund that requires approval. Async handoffs transfer work to a human process, such as escalating a customer case. LLM signals produce structured evidence for downstream decisioning, such as a risk assessment used by a later workflow.

These are first-class workflow patterns, not exceptions. The platform can record what control was triggered, why, who approved, which policy applied, and which catalogue version was active.

---

## 11. Governance and accountability

As AI moves toward autonomous action, organisations need to demonstrate not only what happened, but why it happened. The Fabric is designed to provide evidence for who initiated the request; which capability was selected and why; whether the user was entitled; which capability and catalogue versions were active; what data and tools were accessed; what actions were performed and what approvals were required; which controls and policies applied; and which AI identity performed downstream actions — distinct from the user and from the runtime workload.

This creates a consistent enterprise evidence model across AI capabilities.

---

## 12. Operating model

The enterprise platform owns the Front Door, security, common controls, governance, monitoring, reliability, and standards. Domain teams own specialised AI capabilities, domain workflows, knowledge, and business outcomes. Risk and governance own AI risk, independent oversight, control requirements, compliance, and evidence.

Domain teams innovate independently without rebuilding the enterprise AI platform. Their responsibility is to publish and operate capabilities within the agreed framework. The platform responsibility is to make the safe path the easy path.

---

## 13. What this is not

The Enterprise Agent Fabric is not one giant enterprise agent, a replacement for domain-specific AI capabilities, a central team that owns every AI use case, a requirement for every workload to use the same model, a single runtime for every workload, or a replacement for existing domain platforms.

Instead, the Fabric provides the common enterprise operating model while allowing capabilities to remain specialised and independently evolvable.

---

## 14. Resilience and scale

Enterprise AI capabilities will have different traffic profiles and failure characteristics. Conversational workloads and automated job workloads should not necessarily operate within the same failure domain. In production, chat and API ingress can operate as separate fleets, capabilities can be independently scaled, runtime failures can be isolated, and high-volume workloads can be controlled independently, while the common contract remains consistent across ingress paths.

The principle is that a failure in one AI capability should not become an enterprise-wide AI outage.

---

## 15. V1 scope

V1 establishes the foundational operating pattern rather than attempting to solve every enterprise AI problem. It includes governed enterprise ingress; capability discovery and routing; entitlement and policy enforcement; durable run initiation; a capability registry; a standard capability contract; audit and observability; human approval and handoff patterns; and separation of user, capability, and runtime identities.

It does not attempt to centralise all AI workloads, replace domain AI teams, force a single LLM or model provider, create one runtime for every capability, or eliminate existing domain platforms. The objective is to establish the enterprise pattern, not create a monolithic AI platform.

## 17. Success measures

Success should be measured by business and control outcomes, not by the number of platform components built. Adoption is whether priority capabilities are onboarded through the Fabric and what share of new enterprise AI uses the common pattern. Speed is time to onboard a new governed capability and reduction in duplicated platform engineering. Reuse is the share of capabilities using common enterprise controls and shared services across domains.

Control is coverage of AI actions by entitlement and policy, completeness of audit evidence, and appropriate approval for high-risk actions. Reliability is independent scaling of major workloads, capability-level failure isolation, and platform availability and recovery. Transparency is the ability to reconstruct who asked, what was requested, why the capability was selected, what the AI did, what controls applied, and what the outcome was.

---

## 18. Strategic value

One AI experience simplifies interaction for employees, customers, and applications. A reusable platform speeds deployment of new capabilities. Consistent controls reduce security, governance, and operational risk. Specialised capabilities deliver better domain outcomes than a generic enterprise agent. Enterprise visibility strengthens accountability and auditability. Failure isolation reduces the blast radius of AI incidents. Domain autonomy lets business teams retain ownership and speed.

Taken together, the Fabric allows the organisation to scale AI adoption without scaling fragmentation at the same rate.

---

## 19. Key risks and proposed responses

If the Fabric becomes a central bottleneck, the response is standard contracts and decentralised capability ownership. If the platform drifts toward a monolithic agent runtime, the Fabric control plane stays separate from domain execution. If domain teams bypass the platform, the common path must be easier and faster than building independently. Excessive centralisation is avoided by keeping platform ownership on common controls and domain ownership on business capabilities.

Over-privileged AI actions are addressed by separating user, capability, and runtime permissions. Blast-radius risk is addressed by independent scaling and failure isolation. Governance that would otherwise slow innovation is embedded into the platform rather than applied as manual gates everywhere. The Fabric itself must not become another abstraction layer: the enterprise contract stays simple and implementation-neutral.

---

## 20. Roadmap

V1 establishes the pattern and proves the enterprise operating model: Front Door, capability registry, routing and entitlement, durable execution, a common audit model, human-control patterns, and initial priority capabilities.

Moving from V1 to production hardens the platform for enterprise operation through production security integration, resilience and failure isolation, operational monitoring, capacity management, platform SLOs, governance integration, and a production onboarding model.

V2 scales the ecosystem so onboarding AI capabilities is repeatable across the enterprise, with self-service capability onboarding, expanded policy controls, capability lifecycle management, standardised evaluation, cost and usage optimisation, and broader domain adoption.

## 21. The executive story

![Five beats from fragmented AI and enterprise risk, through one operating model and domain autonomy, to scale with control.](./diagrams/agent-fabric-v1-story.svg)

AI is fragmenting as more teams build more capabilities. That fragmentation creates enterprise risk, because every capability can independently introduce security, governance, integration, and operational complexity. The Fabric creates one operating model — one front door, common controls, and specialised capabilities — while domains keep their autonomy and own their AI outcomes. The enterprise then gets scale with control: AI can grow as a portfolio without an equivalent increase in fragmentation, risk, and complexity.

---

## 22. Final recommendation

The Enterprise Agent Fabric should be adopted as the strategic enterprise pattern for governed AI capability access and operation.

The organisation should not attempt to centralise every AI capability. It should centralise what needs to be consistent — identity, entitlement, routing, policy, governance, audit, observability, and reliability standards — and decentralise what benefits from domain ownership: business capability, domain knowledge, workflows, models, tools, and implementation.

That creates a scalable operating model for enterprise AI: one experience, one front door, many specialised capabilities, and one control model. The Enterprise Agent Fabric becomes the operating foundation that allows the organisation to scale AI as an enterprise capability portfolio rather than a collection of disconnected applications.
