# Enterprise Agent Fabric — Executive TL;DR

## Executive summary (TL;DR)

- Problem: AI is moving from pilots to a portfolio of acting capabilities. Without a common operating model we will face duplicated platform work, inconsistent controls, and governance risk.
- Proposal: Build an Enterprise Agent Fabric — one governed front door that applies central security, policy, audit and observability while domains retain their specialised AI and data.
- Decision requested: Approve a V1 reference pilot (3 months) with an executive sponsor, funding for X FTEs, and success metrics (onboard 3 domains; &lt;2 week onboarding; 90% audit coverage).

---

## Why leadership should care

AI is now production capability — it acts on customer data, money, and regulated processes. If teams build their own entry points, we get repeated platform plumbing and inconsistent controls. The Fabric prevents that by making the safe path the easy path.

## How every request flows (concise)

1. Ask — user or system calls the enterprise front door.
2. Verify — Fabric confirms identity and entitlement.
3. Route — intent routed to the right domain capability under policy.
4. Execute — domain AI runs with approved knowledge and tools.
5. Control — pause, approval, or hand-off for high-risk actions.
6. Complete & audit — outcome returned and evidence retained.

This same path applies to interactive chats and automated jobs.

## What the Fabric standardises

- Security: identity, entitlement, least-privilege for users and capabilities.
- Governance: policy, approval gates, and rules for actions.
- Audit: consistent evidence trail of who asked, what ran, why, and who approved.
- Observability: central operational visibility and alerts across capabilities.
- Evaluation: pre-release checks for safe behaviour.

---

## What domains keep

- Business problems, domain knowledge, models, and outcomes.
- Freedom to choose runtime and specialist logic while integrating with the Fabric contract.

> Centralise controls. Keep intelligence in domains.

---

## Risks & mitigations (concise table)

- Data privacy: restrict data flows, apply least-privilege and data minimisation.
- Unsafe model behaviour: pre-release evaluation, canarying, and human-in-the-loop for risky flows.
- Latency / UX impact: measure policy gating overhead; implement fast-paths for low-risk actions.
- Operational cost: shared platform reduces duplicated spend; budget for observability and SRE.

---

## Success measures (example KPIs)

- Onboarding time per capability (days).
- Number of domain capabilities onboarded (count).
- Percent of user actions covered by central audit (percent).
- Mean time to revoke a capability (minutes).

---

## 3‑phase roadmap (V1 pilot → scale)

- Phase 0 (Weeks 0–2): Executive sponsor, success metrics, pilot domains selected.
- Phase 1 (Weeks 3–12): Build V1 reference Fabric (ingress, entitlement checks, routing, audit schema, basic observability). Onboard 1–3 pilot domains.
- Phase 2 (Months 3–9): Harden controls, add evaluations and approvals, scale onboarding process, automate evidence exports for compliance.

Owners: Enterprise platform (build & run), Domain teams (onboard & own outcomes), Risk & governance (policy & evidence requirements).
